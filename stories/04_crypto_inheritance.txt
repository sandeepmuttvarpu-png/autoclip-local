HEADLINE: MY GREEDY COUSIN TRIED TO STEAL MY GRANDPA'S WILL, BUT DIDN'T KNOW THE PASSWORD
---
When my grandfather passed away, he left me an old encrypted USB drive containing his digital life savings, which everyone assumed was worthless junk. My cousin Kyle immediately demanded the family attorney hand it over, claiming our grandfather was losing his mind when he signed the estate papers. At the probate reading, Kyle made an absolute scene, screaming that I had manipulated an elderly man for money. The attorney smiled calmly, adjusted his glasses, and handed Kyle an envelope containing the public ledger and a cryptographic challenge key. My grandfather was a retired cryptography professor who bought Bitcoin back in 2011. The will explicitly stated that whoever could solve the SHA-256 mnemonic riddle had seven days to claim the private seed phrase. Kyle spent four thousand dollars hiring online hackers to crack it, but within forty-eight hours, every single one told him the mathematical odds were impossible. On day three, I simply typed the first phrase of grandpa's favorite bedtime story from my childhood. The drive unlocked immediately, revealing ninety-four Bitcoin. Kyle threatened to sue me for unjust enrichment, but the judge threw his case out with prejudice. Was this rightful inheritance, or should I have shared it?
