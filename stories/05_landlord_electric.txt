HEADLINE: MY LANDLORD CHARGED ME FOR THE ENTIRE BUILDING'S AIR CONDITIONING, SO I CALLED THE CITY INSPECTOR
---
Last month, my apartment electricity bill spiked from eighty-five dollars to over twelve hundred dollars without any change in my daily routine. When I brought the bill to my landlord, he laughed in my face and told me that rates fluctuate during summer heatwaves and that if I could not pay, I would be evicted by Friday. I knew something was wrong, so I shut off every single circuit breaker in my breaker box and waited by the basement utility meters. Within thirty seconds, the air conditioning units for the leasing office, the gym, and two vacant units upstairs all shut down simultaneously. My landlord had illegally spliced the entire communal HVAC system directly into my personal tenant meter during a renovation six months ago. Instead of confronting him directly, I paid forty dollars to file an emergency complaint with the city building code enforcement and the utility fraud division. The inspector showed up forty-eight hours later with thermal imaging cameras and slapped my landlord with eighteen code violations and a forty-five thousand dollar civil fine. The utility company forced him to reimburse me for eight months of stolen electricity plus triple statutory damages. Did I take it too far, or did he get what was coming to him?
