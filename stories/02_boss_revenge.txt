AITA For Getting My Micromanaging Boss Fired By Following His Exact Orders?

I work as a senior network engineer at a major logistics firm. Last month, our company hired a new director who had zero technical experience but loved power-tripping. He instituted a rule that no engineer was allowed to touch any server, deploy any patch, or fix any outage without his written approval via email first, no matter how urgent.

Two weeks ago on a Friday afternoon at 4:45 PM, our primary warehouse dispatch server threw a critical cache memory fault. If the server wasn't restarted within ten minutes, the entire automated conveyor system would freeze and cost the company two hundred thousand dollars an hour. I immediately emailed the boss explaining the emergency and asked for authorization to restart it.

He replied: "I am leaving the office for the weekend. Do not touch that server under any circumstances until I review the log file on Monday morning." I printed his email, backed it up, clocked out at 5:00 PM, and enjoyed my weekend. By Monday at 8:00 AM, the company had lost over 1.2 million dollars in halted shipments. During the emergency executive board meeting, he tried to blame me for negligence. I calmly projected his email onto the big screen. He was escorted out of the building ten minutes later. AITA?
